## **Supporting Australia’s energy transition** 

Affordably decarbonising Australia’s electricity grid is essential for Australia’s economy. 

As Australia’s economy and population grows, households and businesses electrify, and digitisation increases, the demand for electricity will continue to grow. A reliable and affordable electricity grid is integral to the economy and Australia’s future prosperity. 

Electricity accounts for around 34% of Australia’s emissions. Decarbonising the electricity grid is critical to the decarbonisation of other sectors, including housing – the largest sector that CBA finances. For Australia, and the Bank, to achieve its emissions reduction ambitions, the electricity grid needs to decarbonise. 

Replacing coal-fired power stations with renewables is challenging. The nation needs to address long planning timeframes, supply chain constraints and increase community support for infrastructure. Installing rooftop solar remains popular with consumers. The Government’s renewable energy target provided Australia with direction. Recent policies including the Capacity Investment Scheme and Rewiring the Nation are helping to accelerate the transition. 

With generation capacity and transmission infrastructure progressing, Australia is better placed to meet its 2030 target of a 43% reduction in emissions below 2005 levels. It is our understanding that Australia should be able to produce sufficient generation to meet growing demand over the next decade, provided current State and Federal Government projects to build renewable and dispatchable generation, transmission assets and policies to coordinate consumer energy resources eventuate. Ongoing monitoring, policy adjustments and managing new challenges are essential for helping Australia remain on track. 

Achieving Australia’s emissions targets and producing reliable, affordable energy requires effective community engagement and policies that seek to maintain our country’s energy advantage.

## **Maintaining system reliability and stability** 

Renewable energy generation is increasing. However, high electricity costs remain a concern for consumers and businesses. Australia needs to find affordable ways to achieve its 2030 renewable energy target and maintain system stability. We continue to support AEMO’s view that flexible gas-powered generation is crucial in a high renewable electricity system. Along with batteries and pumped hydro, gas can provide critical backup to variable renewable energy, reliability as coal-fired power stations retire, and flexibility to adapt to demand changes. Gas plants, capable of rapid startup, help to provide continuous electricity supply and support grid reliability and stability, especially during high-demand periods and extreme weather. 

Australians broadly support a focus on the nation’s climate change ambitions but are concerned about the impact on their cost of living and quality of life. The transition requires government and industry to balance and communicate competing trade-offs between energy sources. Effective system management and grid stability will continue to be important to maximise use of renewables while preventing potential blackouts. 

## **Reducing emissions in key sectors** 

Australia’s Climate Change Authority (CCA) has published six sector pathways.[ 1] The CCA’s report identifies potential technologies and actions for each sector that, taken together, could deliver net zero emissions for Australia by 2050. 

The CCA recognises that emissions must fall across all sectors, however each sector is on a different pathway to net zero by 2050. For some sectors, like power generation, with coordinated planning, the pathway may be steep and rapid as technologies are mature and ready to scale. Sectors like agriculture, however, are expected to experience a slower and more gradual path as technologies are in early development. Government policy support and investment are needed to accelerate the deployment of early-stage technologies and actions.
