## **Nature and biodiversity** 

We continue to recognise the interconnection between nature and climate issues, and the importance of Australia taking action to maintain, restore and enhance nature and biodiversity. 

## **Progressing our approach to nature** 

With one in four Australian businesses calling us their main financial institution, we play an important role in financing economic activity. Sectors with greater exposure to nature-related risks, such as agriculture, mining and construction, are important to building Australia’s future economy and need balanced, long-term support to manage their impacts and dependencies. 

## **Assessing naturerelated impacts in our own operations** 

As a large Australian lender to the housing and commercial property sectors, we believe we have a role to play in understanding the built environment’s impact and dependency on nature, starting with our own operations. 

Understanding our customers’ impacts and dependencies on nature can help us manage the risks and opportunities these create. Our 2023 ENCORE analysis identified agriculture and mining as priority sectors, as well as the built environment in relation to our operations. 

We continued to monitor our water and waste use which were identified as the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD) metrics most material to our operations. As part of our ongoing efforts to reduce waste to landfill, we trialled reusable coffee cups, expanded the roll out of coffee cup sorting stations, upgraded our waste infrastructure and signage, and introduced a dry waste stream into our Sydney commercial offices this year. 

Since then, we have been building internal analytical capabilities, exploring emerging tools and piloting elements of the ‘LEAP’ (locate, evaluate, assess and prepare) framework. While data collection and measurement methods continue to evolve, there is a clear need for nationally consistent definitions, metrics and datasets tailored to the Australian context. 

We welcome the Australian Federal Government’s initiative to enhance environmental information through the newly established statutory body, Environment Information Australia. We continue to monitor developments under Australia’s Nature Positive Plan. We welcome further work across government and key industry stakeholder groups to develop nationally consistent tools and datasets that could support the pragmatic development of nature commitments and consider the interdependencies with climate objectives. 

As a continuation of our embodied carbon assessments, we engaged a third-party consultant to conduct an assessment into the impacts on nature from our retail fitout. The assessment identified the highest impact materials on nature as aluminium, steel and timber and further assessment of the supply chains of these materials were carried out. We continue to explore how these findings can be integrated into design decisions. 

## **Continuing to develop our measurement capabilities** 

We continued to build analytical capabilities to assess nature impacts and dependencies for parts of our lending portfolio. This includes taking opportunities to partner with organisations who can support better measurement of nature-related metrics and support customers in working towards improved nature outcomes.

This year we built on previous geospatial analysis and continued to explore publicly available datasets and methodologies to assess where our operations and lending activities intersect with nature. This exercise identified challenges in timeliness and accuracy of publicly available information on the location of relevant assets. Through our climate stress test, we also explored exposure to water stress for select regions in our agriculture portfolio. 

We also participated in an Early Adopter Program with CSIRO to test their new NatureIQ tool that combines multiple CSIRO environmental indicators, models and datasets. The tool supports assessment and monitoring of indicators such as ecosystem condition and species extinction risk.

## **Governance** 

The Board continues to oversee the assessment of risks and opportunities for the Bank arising from climate change. 

## **The Board’s oversight of climate issues** 

The Board’s responsibilities include considering the material E&S impacts of the Bank’s activities. Each of the Board’s four standing Committees assists the Board to carry out its responsibilities, with the Audit and Risk & Compliance Committees primarily assisting the Board with its oversight of climate issues. 

The Board approves the Group E&S Policy and Framework, and oversees the management of climate risk within the Bank’s overall business strategy and risk appetite. The Board also monitors the E&S work program, which includes the development and delivery of CBA’s climate strategy and progress against goals and targets. The Board and the Audit and Risk & Compliance Committees regularly receive reports from management on climate-related matters. The contents, including management’s recommendations, are noted, discussed and/or approved.
