## **Tracking towards our Sustainability Funding Target** 

As at 30 June 2025, we have provided $64.4 billion in cumulative funding towards our Sustainability Funding Target (SFT) of $70 billion by 2030. Given the recent release of the Australian Sustainable Finance Taxonomy and in light of reaching more than 92% of our 2030 target this year, we will review our target and eligibility criteria next year. ASB separately tracks and measures the funding they provide towards their SFT. Since 2022 ASB has provided a cumulative NZ $2.1 billion in funding towards their target of NZ $6.5 billion by 2030. 

**For more information on our SFT criteria, see pages 416–418 in the** _**Climate disclosures appendix**_ **.**

## **Capital deployment** 

Capital deployment relating to climate-related risks and opportunities may be categorised into the following: 

**Financing:** Climate-related financing refers to the offering of lending products, credit facilities, or other financing solutions that support customers in addressing their climate-related risks or pursuing their climate-related opportunities. Our SFT monitors the level of cumulative funding within the scope of our SFT eligibility criteria. 

**Investment:** Climate-related investment refers to capital deployed through financial instruments or equity stakes in public or private markets that relate to the Bank’s climate-related risks or opportunities and where climate considerations form a material part of the investment rationale or potential outcomes. This year CBA invested $46 million in climate-related investments. 

**Expenditure:** Climate-related expenditure refers to capital or operational spending incurred in managing climate-related risks or pursuing climate-related opportunities. This includes direct costs related to internal systems, infrastructure, tools or processes that are focused on climate risk mitigation, adaptation or resilience. No material direct climate-related expenditure was noted this year. Indirect costs include ongoing expenditure which would be incurred ordinarily, making it impractical to allocate costs as specifically climate-related. These indirect costs include activities such as branch and office refurbishments where we are actively incorporating sustainable practices through branch design and energy efficient retrofits; the ATM refresh project which incorporates lower carbon, natural and renewable materials; and the resourcing costs associated with ESG teams who work to deliver our climate strategy.
