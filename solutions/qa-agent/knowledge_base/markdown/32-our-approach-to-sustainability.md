## **Pursuing excellence in our Using customer customer experiences feedback** 

We aim to consistently provide superior customer experiences by understanding our customers’ needs and expectations. 

**Our customers expect simple, seamless experiences. While we aim to get things right every time, when things go wrong, how we respond matters. Feedback and complaints are valuable opportunities to listen, learn and improve.** 

## **Learning from customer feedback** 

Customer insights from a range of sources help us understand what is working well and where there are opportunities to improve. 

**In response to customer dissatisfaction around fraudulent transactions, we have increased fraud alerts to 35,000 per day and integrated them with a self-service tool. We now identify the majority of fraudulent transactions and enable customers to lodge disputes in a matter of minutes without the need to call or message us or go to a branch.** 

We use customer experience surveys to capture feedback, monitor trends and understand how customers engage with us. We also monitor operational metrics, including complaints, disputes, wait times and service availability, providing useful indicators of areas requiring attention. These metrics together with the Net Promoter Score (NPS), an external measure of customer advocacy, give us a benchmark to improve on. 

We are prioritising key experiences across consumer and small business banking and are embedding a new approach to collecting direct customer feedback. This approach seeks to provide a more complete and consistent understanding of customer experiences, helping us better focus on outcomes for our customers. 

**Feedback from customers, particularly those unable to visit a branch, also led us to digitise the lodgement process for Power Of Attorneys and Financial Management Orders. This inclusive shift has improved efficiency and convenience, with over 90% of lodgements now made online.** 

We are also exploring ways to gain deeper customer insights. We have now entered our second year of a program that to-date has observed financial behaviours and decisions inside the homes of over 70 young adult Australians across 10 locations. Early insights indicate young adults often rely on non-bank tools to manage finances, revealing unmet needs with their primary bank. 

## **Measuring customer experience** 

We currently hold the #1 NPS ranking across major banks in the Consumer, Business and Digital categories, and have held this ranking across all for at least 11 months. This year our Institutional NPS ranking has dropped to #2 after previously holding the #1 ranking for 24 consecutive months. There is still more work to do to improve our NPS scores. Detailed reports on the drivers of NPS can be accessed by our people to understand what is influencing the absolute scores. This change highlights the importance of focusing on continuously improving our service and prioritising the customer in everything we do.

## **Making banking safer for our customers** 

**==> picture [122 x 245] intentionally omitted <==**

Fraud, scams, cyber threats and financial crime continue to impact many Australians. We have more work to do and remain focused on investing to help protect our customers and communities. 

More customers than ever are choosing to engage with us through the CommBank app. We are balancing transaction speed with the need for a streamlined, safe and secure digital payment experience. New warnings for first-time payments through NetBank and the CommBank app now provide enhanced alerts for customers. Increased alerting helped to reduce customer-reported fraud by 40% over the year. 

We also introduced a feature in the CommBank app that allows customers to see all digital wallets their payment details have been linked to and remove any they do not recognise. More than 1.3 million customers have used this feature since launch in August 2024. We have seen a 30% reduction in customer scam losses year-on-year supported by initiatives including behavioural security, NameCheck, CallerCheck and CustomerCheck. 

To further strengthen account protection, we introduced a new multi-factor authentication process for NetBank, requiring customers to confirm login attempts via the app. This added security layer helps prevent unauthorised access, even in cases where a user’s password has been comprised.

## **Supporting financial inclusion** 

As Australia’s largest bank, it is important that we provide tailored and accessible solutions to promote the financial inclusion of our customers and communities. 

## **Improving access to banking** 

We aim to provide products and services that meet the diverse needs of our customers and provide equitable access to help them achieve their financial goals. We recognise there are systemic barriers to banking and financial inclusion experienced by some members of the community, and we are committed to understanding and removing these barriers so more Australians can access banking confidently, independently and with dignity. 

We are fortunate to have one in two new migrants to Australia trust us with their banking needs and recognise the unique challenges they face. This year we updated and improved our Moving to Australia online resources, which provide practical guidance on opening bank accounts, managing money and accessing digital tools – designed to support a confident start to life in Australia. 

To better support our customers who hold eligible government concession cards, we offer a basic bank account, our Streamline Basic account, which has no monthly account keeping, overdraw or dishonour fees. 

Improving financial inclusion for First Nations customers and communities remains a priority. Alongside our branch teams and Indigenous Customer Assistance Line, Indigenous Business Banking team, and Indigenous banking concierge offer support tailored to community needs. 

We are also focused on building strong financial awareness and habits in children. Kit, our pocket money app for kids, used by over 100,000 customers, helps improve financial capability through fun and interactive learning. This year we introduced linking between interest-earning CommBank Youthsaver accounts and Kit, allowing kids to transfer and see their money grow. We also offer Minor Trust accounts through CommSec, allowing parents to hold investments on behalf of their children to help build their financial future. 

## **Continuing to support our regional communities** 

**==> picture [166 x 249] intentionally omitted <==**

We continue to support regional Australia with over 40% of our branch network located in regional areas. We maintained the biggest branch network in regional Australia and extended the CBA-branded regional branch closure moratorium until the end of July 2027. Our Bank@Post partnership with Australia Post also helps our customers, especially those in rural and regional areas, to continue having access to face-to-face banking services. There are more than 3,300 Bank@Post outlets across Australia, including in over 1,100 communities where there is no bank branch of any kind. 

To deepen our relationship with regional customers and communities we continued our regional growth initiative. This initiative connects business bankers with our branch network to support regional advocacy, customer engagement and brand awareness. CBA also sponsored the National Bush Summit, a national campaign advocating for regional and rural Australia, addressing important issues like cost of living, fraud and scams, and the future of farming and agriculture. 

We also aim to help more regional Australians stay safe online. We offered regional Australians access to more than 600 ‘Staying Safe Online’ education sessions this year. By partnering with local community groups to expand accessibility to the program, we delivered more than 300 of these free sessions in community centres in regional towns allowing more people to attend, no matter who they bank with. 

## **Recognising and supporting customers with hidden disabilities** 

As part of the Bank’s ongoing commitment to deliver more inclusive products and experiences, we joined the Hidden Disabilities Sunflower program. A discreet symbol, worn as a pin, lanyard or wristband, allows people to voluntarily share with others that they have a disability or condition that may not be immediately apparent, and that they may need extra support. We provide training to our retail frontline teams to help them recognise and support customers with a hidden disability or non-visible condition, and we encourage our customers to wear the symbol if they feel comfortable to do so. The Hidden Disabilities Sunflower program complements our Equal Access Toolkit, used in all CBA branches and includes practical tools to support customers who may need an adjusted experience to complete a task in branch independently. We have also recently launched a new webpage for our neurodivergent customers. 

**See more about neuroinclusive banking at commbank.com.au/neuroinclusivebanking**
