## **Committee considerations** 

- The LTAR was introduced in the 2021 financial year when the executive remuneration framework was redesigned to significantly reduce total remuneration opportunity (a reduction of up to 19%) and to increase the proportion of total remuneration deferred over longer time horizons (currently up to five years for GEs and six years for the CEO), in line with CPS 511. 

**Long-term alignment remuneration award (LTAR)** 

- The framework is compliant with CPS 511 which requires a material weighting to non-financial measures for variable remuneration. 

- Together the LTAR and LTVR support a collective focus on sustainable shareholder value creation, with the LTAR performance assessment considering consistent performance in leadership and strategic execution, reflecting long-term risk and performance outcomes. 

- The Board completes an annual pre-grant assessment to determine whether executive performance has met expectations or if performance warrants a reduction to the LTAR grant value. A pre-vest assessment was formally introduced in the 2023 financial year to further strengthen the focus on non-financial outcomes over the long term as a driver of sustainable shareholder value creation. 

- While the FY22 LTAR award does not formally require a pre-vest assessment, the Board conducted a look-back review over the four-year LTAR restriction period and determined that no adjustments were required and the FY22 LTAR vested in full.

**2025 remuneration changes** 

CBA’s executive remuneration framework is designed to attract and retain exceptional executive talent, align with and support sustainable shareholder returns, and meet regulatory requirements. 

The Board reviews the competitiveness of executive remuneration on an annual basis, taking into account role scope and scale, and incumbent capability and experience. Competitiveness is assessed via external benchmarking, primarily with consideration of other Australian peer banks (ANZ Group (ANZ), National Australia Bank (NAB) and Westpac Banking Corporation (WBC)) and large ASX listed companies, with supplementary consideration of remuneration in global jurisdictions, particularly where we have sourced or are at risk of losing executive talent. 

Our ELT has an average tenure of four years in a GE role, with valuable executive experience providing strong, stable leadership for the Bank. The team has a track record of delivering sustainable performance and significant value for shareholders, with TSR of 220% delivered over the past five years. In this context, maintaining competitive remuneration is critical to improve our ELT’s market position against both Australian and relevant offshore comparator groups. 

In Australia and offshore, we have observed competition for talent for various critical functions and in senior layers of management, including below executive KMP level. Despite anticipating increasing competition for talent, we have experienced some regrettable turnover to Australian peer banks. 

Following a comprehensive market review, the Board implemented a number of FR increases across the ELT during the 2025 financial year. These increases are designed to help improve competitiveness and our ability to retain the executive talent required to deliver our strategy and achieve continued high performance and long-term value creation. 

## **Other executive KMP remuneration changes** 

- FR increases were provided to six other executive KMP during the 2025 financial year. 

- No increase was provided to any other executive KMP in the 2024 financial year. 

- Prior to the increases, FR was typically positioned at rank two or three against Australian peer banks. 

- FR increases ranged from 7.0% to 16.7%, with an average increase of 10.1%, improving the competitiveness of FR. Since the 2021 financial year, the compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of average FR increases was 2.5%.

## **CEO remuneration changes** 

- The FR increase for the CEO recognises his experience, sustained performance and leadership impact since his appointment in 2018. The increase also reflects consideration of CBA’s size, scale and market capitalisation, and emerging market trends. 

- For the 2025 financial year, the CEO’s FR was increased from $2.50m to $2.85m. 

- The CEO’s FR has not been increased for three years, with the last FR increase provided being 8.7%, effective in the 2022 financial year. 

- In the 2025 financial year, the CEO’s total remuneration received was $7.02m, significantly lower than in FY24 ($8.98m) and FY23 ($10.43m), reflecting the increased deferral and holding periods introduced for the FY21 LTAR and LTVR to meet CPS 511 requirements. 

- The FR increase positions the CEO’s total maximum remuneration (TMR) appropriately against the relevant domestic banking comparators, commensurate with CBA’s relative size and scale, while still below the median of other CEOs of the ASX comparator group, and in the lower quartile against the offshore comparator group.

## **Other changes to remuneration arrangements** 

Technology is a critical enabler of CBA’s strategic priorities. Gavin Munroe (Group Chief Information Officer) was granted a one-off award comprising of $1.50m of 9,681 performance rights to recognise his responsibility for delivery of agreed technology priorities over a three-year period. These priorities are designed to support the modernisation of CBA’s technology estate; strengthen technology leadership; enable greater stability, security, and continuity; enable the utilisation of new tools, technologies and AI; and enhance customer experience. Performance measures underpinning the award include: 

- Transition of technology to a security-first framework across CBA; 

- Delivery of agreed technology execution priorities; and 

- Model deployment, standards and technology requirements for AI across business lines, and acceleration of software development times. 

The vesting outcome will be determined by the Board in 2027 and 2028 through an objective assessment of the performance measures that reflect the successful delivery of key outcomes in CBA’s technology strategy agenda. 

Each performance right entitles Gavin to receive one CBA share, subject to vesting conditions. 

The number of performance rights that vest following the performance assessment could range from 0% to 100%. The performance rights do not receive dividends (or dividend-equivalent payments). The performance rights are subject to the same malus and clawback rules as the LTAR and LTVR awards.
