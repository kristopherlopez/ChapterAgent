## **Acute event scenarios** 

These three acute scenarios were selected as they are considered severe, but plausible climate-related events that could impact the Bank’s profitability and cash flows in any given year. 

**Long-term drought impacting the agriculture portfolio** 

**Series of severe cyclones over South-East Queensland** 

**Extended electricity outage in New South Wales** 

Assumes a severe drought across large areas of Australia lasting for 10 years. There is a simultaneous rise in interest rates due to unrelated economic factors, stable commodity prices globally and declines in farm property values in the high drought impacted areas. 

Assumes two severe cyclones make landfall in the South-East QLD region in consecutive years. This results in significant property damage, supply chain disruptions, property price declines, declining insurance affordability and an economic decline in QLD. 

Assumes two ageing coal-fired power stations in NSW become inoperable for up to six months. This results in rolling blackouts which impact productivity of high energy usage industries.

## **Why we ran this scenario** 

Research indicates that large, populated areas across South-East QLD and Northern NSW may experience more severe cyclones in the future as cyclones begin to track further south. We ran this scenario to enhance our understanding of the potential losses to the Bank from an extreme weather event that may become increasingly plausible over time. 

## **Key scenario assumptions** 

The scenario assumes two severe cyclones (Category 4) make landfall over South-East QLD a year apart. First over the Sunshine Coast and then Brisbane. Each of these Category 4 cyclones are currently individually assessed as 1-in-200-year events, however the probability of two severe cyclone events in successive years in this location is currently extremely low. This scenario results in significant property damage from wind, storm surges and inland cyclone-induced flooding. This is exacerbated where properties are not built to withstand Category 4 cyclone winds. Limited government support is assumed. Supply chain disruptions and downtime impact business productivity. Following the back-to-back cyclones, property prices decline in impacted areas and the events lead to an economic decline in QLD with unemployment rates increasing materially, particularly in regional areas.

## **Key scenario assumptions** 

The scenario assumes a severe drought lasting for 10 years across parts of the Murray-Darling Basin and Western Australia. The scenario assumes farm values decline in the high-drought impact areas, the drought occurs during a high interest rate environment for reasons unrelated to the drought and the effectiveness of government support declines over the scenario. 

## **Insights from our analysis** 

Although not modelled in this scenario, losses would likely be further exacerbated if impacts to nature are considered. Prolonged drought conditions degrade nature and critical ecosystem services required by farming systems. For example, drought can cause a loss of soil and sediment retention and loss of vegetation that reduces soil quality and increases risk of soil erosion, desertification and soil salinity, further impacting agricultural productivity and resilience. 

Total losses to the Bank over the 10 years are assessed to be below – $500 million, split relatively evenly between farming mainly grain growing, mixed farming and beef cattle farming – and agriculture adjacent industries, mainly from food manufacturing and farm produce wholesaling. The maximum loss in any individual year under this scenario does not exceed $100 million.

## **Why we ran this scenario** 

AEMO noted in the 2024 ISP that 90% of Australia’s coalfired power generators are forecast to retire by 2035, and are becoming less reliable and more difficult to maintain. Investment is needed urgently and new generation, storage and firming must be in place before coal-fired power stations retire. We ran this scenario to enhance our understanding of the potential losses to the Bank from a long-term electricity outage caused by two coal-fired power stations becoming simultaneously inoperable. 

## **Insights from our analysis** 

This combination of increased wholesale electricity prices and reduced production leads to estimated credit losses to the Bank from the manufacturing sector of below $200 million over five years. The greatest losses arise from the manufacturing sub-industries of transport equipment, machinery and equipment, food and chemical manufacturing. Energy price increases of 30% in retail do not drive material retail credit losses for the Bank, with losses estimated to be below $100 million driven by a slight economic downturn.
