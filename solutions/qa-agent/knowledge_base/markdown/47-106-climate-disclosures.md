## **Our role in helping to build Australia’s future economy** 

Since 2009 we have progressively reduced the emissions from our operations, for example the energy that powers our branches and buildings. This now makes up less than 1% of our total emissions. However, the emissions associated with the lending we provide to our customers is more than 99% of our total emissions. Importantly, much of the emissions produced by our customers and suppliers come from activities or actions that are not within the Bank’s direct control or are more challenging to influence. 

Climate change can have a significant impact on the Australian economy and our communities. Given one in three Australians and one in four Australian businesses call us their main financial institution, our role is to help build Australia’s future economy by supporting our customers and communities as they navigate the choices they need to make to transition to a lower-carbon economy. 

While climate change is not currently estimated to be financially material to the Bank in the short term, it is important that our climate strategy aligns to our purpose. This means supporting our customers as they take action to adapt to and/or mitigate the physical or transition impacts of climate change. Further, the risks we may be exposed to or the opportunities that may be available to us need to be assessed, quantified where possible, and have an appropriate level of control and oversight by management and the Board. 

We acknowledge encouraging behavioural shifts or action on climate change can be challenging, take time and requires trust. As we approach 2030 our focus will be on working with our customers and suppliers to help them understand the actions they can take to transition and build their resilience to climate impacts.

## **Financing Australia’s transition to net zero by 2050** 

Our in-scope drawn lending exposed to the Australian housing sector accounts for 63.9% of total in-scope drawn lending and makes up 17.3% of our total financed emissions. The remaining portfolio consists mostly of business lending across a diverse range of sectors and customers, ranging from small businesses to institutional customers. Lending to upstream oil and gas extraction, and thermal coal mining accounts for <0.1% of total in-scope drawn lending and is 2.0% of our financed emissions. Lending to the power generation sector represents 0.5% of our in-scope drawn lending and 1.9% of our financed emissions. CBA has set sector-level financed emissions goals and targets on sectors that account for 71% of the Bank’s in-scope drawn lending.[ 1]

While we continue to offer emergency assistance and tailored support options to our customers and communities, we acknowledge that our efforts have been focused on short-term, post-event support. We are supportive of government assistance that considers long-term outcomes, investment in climate-resilient infrastructure and home retrofitting programs. 

Australia is making progress in transitioning to net zero. However we acknowledge certain stakeholders may be disproportionately impacted. This includes businesses and households who may struggle to switch to loweremissions technologies and regions impacted by changes in employment opportunities. These challenges can be amplified by the physical impacts of climate change. 

Lower-income households, renters and apartment residents may find it more difficult to participate in the home energy transition due to costs, split incentives or practical infrastructure challenges. We are supportive of State and/or Federal Government policies that consider an inclusive transition and address the needs of more vulnerable communities. 

We are building our data and analytical capabilities to better understand the impact of climate on our customers. This year we launched the Natural Hazard feature pilot in the CommBank app using the New South Wales (NSW) Rural Fire Service’s bushfire dataset. The pilot enables customers to understand whether their property may be located on bushfire prone land to support their adaptation and mitigation actions.
