## **Upstream oil and gas extraction** 

**In-scope drawn lending as a proportion of Group TCE <0.1%** 

## **Sector overview and outlook** 

As at 30 June 2024, lending to the upstream oil and gas extraction sectors represented <0.1% of our in-scope drawn lending and 1.9% of our financed emissions. In 2023, Scope 1 emissions from upstream oil and gas extraction accounted for approximately 10% of Australia’s total emissions.1 These industries also have significant downstream emissions due to the combustion of the products they produce. 

In AEMO’s 2024 ISP, as coal-fired power stations retire, gas-powered generation is expected to continue playing a role in supporting Australia’s economic activity through the transition. Gas-powered electricity generation can be used to provide firming support to variable renewable energy when other dispatchable sources are unavailable. Gas is also used in a range of industrial applications where lower carbon alternatives are not yet commercially available, particularly those requiring heat over 500°C. AEMO’s 2025 Gas Statement of Opportunities forecasts shortfall risks under peak conditions from 2028, three years later than forecast in 2024. AEMO’s update reinforces the need to complete committed and anticipated production, storage and infrastructure developments, amongst other measures.

## **Assumptions and risks in achieving our targets** 

The reference scenarios that inform our targets assume a gradual decline in demand for oil and gas, noting the need for dispatchable capacity, which could include gas-powered firming generation, in the future. This decline is complemented by a small share of emissions being captured through carbon capture, use and storage.[ 4] In Australia, reductions in emissions in the oil and gas sector are assumed to be supported by the Federal Government’s Safeguard Mechanism, which incentivises solutions to minimise Scope 1 emissions, including through carbon capture, use and storage. 

## **Actions we are taking** 

We support customers in these sectors by providing lending in line with our E&S Framework. The Framework outlines the minimum requirements we have set for financing activities for certain customers in these sectors. We also monitor the impact of any proposed new transactions in these sectors on our 2030 upstream oil and gas extraction targets. Certain customers in these sectors are also expected to have published a Transition Plan. 

**For important information on our actions and the commitments we have made to customers operating in these sectors, see pages 114–115. See our refreshed 2025 E&S Framework for more information at commbank.com.au/policies**

## **Actions we are taking** 

We expect to achieve our 2030 target through a managed exit of our international institutional banking exposures. Any new origination in the shipping sector is intended to be focused on Australian transport, trade and infrastructure. 

The reference scenario that informs our target assumes low-carbon fuels, such as bioenergy and ammonia, begin to account for at least 15% of energy consumed by shipping by 2030. 

Decarbonising the shipping sector is largely dependent on efficiency improvements including a mixture of those already available, in addition to new technologies being developed and switching to low-carbon fuels. Should the scaling of low-carbon fuels not happen or not happen at the rate anticipated, or if policy settings are not supportive, then achieving our target may be unlikely.
