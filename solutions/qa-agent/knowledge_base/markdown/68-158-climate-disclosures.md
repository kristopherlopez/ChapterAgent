## **Current and anticipated impacts on our financial statements** 

## **Current year impact on our financial statements** 

The Bank monitors its financial performance, financial position and cashflow on an ongoing basis through its governance structure. Climate-related effects can be widespread and interrelated, and may influence our operations, strategy and financial outcomes over the short, medium and long term. As per the result of our CRMA, no climate-related risks or opportunities were noted this year that have had a material impact on the Bank’s financial performance, financial position or cash flows. 

## **Anticipated impacts on our financial statements** 

In some years, the Bank may experience increases in credit losses due to extreme weather events, however the scenario results indicate that financial impacts to the Bank from climate-related impacts are not expected to be material. This reflects the nature of our operations, the sectors in which we operate, and our existing risk management frameworks and portfolio diversification, which together provide a degree of resilience against potential climate-related impacts. 

The Bank is well placed to identify and pursue available opportunities arising from Australia’s transition to net zero by 2050. For example, the development of sustainable finance products, tools and services to support customers to transition and adapt to the impacts of climate change. 

While climate change presents challenges to the Australian economy and our communities, our core business remains resilient. A relatively small proportion of the Group’s TCE is currently exposed to high or very high physical or transition risk. Our customers have historically demonstrated strong repayment performance and we have established support mechanisms to assist them in times of financial stress, including those potentially arising from climate-related events. Although severe climate-related events could result in an increase in single year credit losses, these losses are assessed as a medium risk to the Bank as they would not result in a breach of our current capital, liquidity or profitability RAS indicators. This reflects our strong provisioning levels and capital position.

## **Other climate-related risk assessment tools** 

## **Climate stress index for agriculture** 

In May 2024, we launched the climate stress index (CSI) tool for our bankers, developed in collaboration with CSIRO. The CSI tool forecasts climate stress through to 2030 by postcode, based on key agri-climatic productivity variables across grain, livestock and dairy sectors. It provides a composite score for each postcode-commodity combination indicating whether climate stress is expected to decrease, remain stable, or increase moderately or at a high rate. The tool enhances our understanding of the degree, nature and location of chronic climate risks to our lending securities. Currently, 19% of the Bank’s relevant agricultural exposures are secured by properties located in postcodes with forecasted increasing rates of climate 

stress. The tool enables our bankers to have conversations with customers on their long-term climate adaptation plans relevant for credit risk assessment. CSI insights are also used for portfolio monitoring in livestock, grain and dairy commodities, helping us track exposure trends and inform strategic planning. Insights from the tool can be shared with customers through a Climate Insight Report, tailored to their region and commodities, to help them consider the data and plan for future climate-related resilience. We will retire our productivity mapping tool given the launch of the CSI which provides a forward view of climate risk. We will continue to assess tools and capabilities for our agriculture customers’ climate risk assessments. 

## **Supplier risk governance tool** 

In June 2023 we introduced the environmental risk assessment in the supplier risk governance (SRG) tool. As part of CBA’s annual review, industry environmental risk ratings are updated to support the inherent environmental risk assessment of our suppliers. For suppliers in industries with high risk ratings, assessments are undertaken when onboarding for new services, or variation or renewal of an existing service, and form part of supplier risk screening before contracts are executed. In 2024, in addition to the assessments that were completed when onboarding suppliers in high-risk industries, we triggered the environmental risk assessment for our leasing agents and landlords, and our top 100 suppliers by spend. As a result, the number of suppliers assessed decreased from last year and now totals 71. 

Of these suppliers, all reported they had no regulatory environmental breaches over the past three years.
