## **Improving our materiality assessment** 

Our annual materiality assessment seeks to identify sustainability topics that are most relevant to our stakeholders and CBA. Insights from the assessment are shared across the Bank and with the Board, to help inform decision making and our sustainability disclosures. 

**Our materiality assessment process Identify initial longlist of topics Review operating context, industry trends, peer insights, macrotrends, sustainability trends and regulatory environment. 1 Filter list for relevance Conduct internal analysis and interviews with internal subject matter experts. Align with sustainability and materiality assessment frameworks to capture material topics. 2 Filter list for importance Internal subject matter experts rate topics by (1) ability to impact the economy, environment and people; (2) CBA’s ability to influence the topic; and (3) financial materiality. Use the Bank’s 5x5 risk matrix to guide ratings. 3 Filter list for materiality Senior management review and validate prioritised topics considering both impact and financial materiality. Group topics into material themes. 4 Report on material themes Share insights across the Bank and with the Board. Use material themes to guide annual reporting, disclosing material sustainability-related themes which matter most to our key stakeholders and CBA. 5** 

The assessment considers CBA’s operating environment and uses insights from our stakeholder engagement. Internal subject matter experts, representing key stakeholder groups, assess proposed topics based on their direct engagement with stakeholders. Each topic is evaluated for its relevance to Australia’s economy, environment and people, and CBA’s impact on or ability to influence the topic (impact materiality), as well as the topic’s potential impact on CBA’s financial performance (financial materiality). 

Guided by global frameworks such as the Global Reporting Initiative and Sustainability Accounting Standards Board, our materiality assessment requires significant judgement and is a qualitative assessment. It considers a range of sustainability topics through research, stakeholder interviews and workshops with senior management. 

This year we enhanced our materiality assessment process to better identify topics that are potentially financially material for CBA. We also aligned our impact assessment scale to the Bank’s 5x5 risk matrix, enabling us to assess and prioritise material topics more consistently. Our assessment will continue to evolve in line with emerging guidance to improve how we identify material sustainability-related impacts, risks and opportunities. 

## **Understanding this year’s material themes** 

The new Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards present an opportunity for us to enhance how we address material sustainability issues, while improving the transparency, consistency and comparability of our sustainability disclosures over time. 

Our material themes and underlying topics remain aligned with the strategic priorities, material risks and themes outlined in our 2024 Annual Report. 

Customer experience, support and community impact continue to be top priorities for both stakeholders and CBA. This year underlying topics such as fraud and scams, cost of living, payments, customer experience and housing affordability gained importance. Access to banking also rose in importance, driven by an increased focus on cash and branch availability in regional Australia. 

Our report is structured around material themes that reflect our most significant sustainability topics for the year. The theme ‘Banking strategy, operations and operating context’ is not presented as a standalone material theme. While core to the Bank’s strategy and financial performance, we have not classified it as a sustainability topic and it is not expected to be assessed as a separate sustainability theme in future years. Given the theme is inherently material to the Bank, our approach and activities are disclosed in other sections of this report. 

Stakeholders reaffirmed that a supported and engaged workforce is essential to CBA’s value creation. CBA plays a key role in shaping employee engagement and wellbeing, and fostering a workplace culture that is safe, respectful and inclusive. As digitisation and AI reshape the workforce, the need for reskilling and upskilling is growing, though stakeholders also noted people skills remain vital for customer-facing roles.
