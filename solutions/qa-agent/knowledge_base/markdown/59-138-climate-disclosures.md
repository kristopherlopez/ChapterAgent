## **Management accountability** 

The Board has delegated the management of the Bank to the CEO, except for certain powers retained by the Board or delegated to others including Board and Management Committees. Once the Board has approved the strategic objectives and risk appetite of the Bank, which includes our climate strategy, the CEO is accountable for execution. The CEO then delegates authority to the Group Executives for the effective execution of the Bank’s operational activities.

## **Embedding climate governance within our business** 

Governance committees within the Bank, such as risk management committees, product governance forums and transaction-level committees, support the Board’s oversight and the Executive Leadership Team’s (ELT’s) management of climate-related risks and opportunities. Each of these committees and forums have charters that govern and outline roles and responsibilities. Climate-related financial risks and opportunities can be escalated through these reporting structures. Governance processes are supported by the application of a range of internal policies, standards and procedures that determine the way we deliver our products and services. For example, our Product Development and Distribution Standard embeds consideration of E&S product features into the product governance process, which includes oversight of changes to product features and periodic reviews through an E&S lens.

## **Transaction oversight for Institutional Banking and Markets customers** 

In RBS, we take a portfolio managed approach to E&S governance, implementing this for products, tools or features that we provide to customers, rather than through individual customer ESG risk assessments. For new CBA product offerings with E&S features, including the Home Energy Upgrades service, the Bank carefully considered the potential impacts to our conduct, strategic and credit risks. Before being sent, customer communications are reviewed to check environmental claims are accurate and can be supported. 

In IB&M, lending customers are relationship managed and supported directly by our bankers. As part of the corporate lending process, ESG risk assessments are completed for institutional customers, and are reviewed and refreshed annually. IB&M operates two key forums that support consideration of E&S risks and other reputational risks. These review forums, which involve senior management from within and outside IB&M, include an E&S Deal Review (EDR) and the IB&M Commitments Committee. 

Before credit approval, a proposed transaction is escalated to EDR if it meets certain conditions, including if the customer’s activities are captured in the relevant fossil fuel financingrelated commitments in our E&S Framework; the transaction may impact a sector-level target and/or exposure reporting for certain sectors; or the ESG risk assessment results in a heightened ESG risk rating. Where appropriate, the EDR can also escalate a proposed transaction to the IB&M Commitments Committee, particularly where it may impact the Bank’s non-financial risks, reputational risk, strategy or values. If the transaction is supported for approval, it then proceeds through the credit approval pathway. 

## **Our approach to transaction governance in Business Banking** 

In BB, our approach to climate-related governance is determined by the size of a customer’s current or proposed commercial or corporate lending. Customers with greater than or equal to $1.5 million in lending require an ESG risk assessment. The majority of these customers are relationship managed by our bankers. Depending on the customer’s ESG risk profile, or where additional oversight is required, ESG risk assessments may be escalated to senior management or the BB Commitments Committee. Matters escalated to the BB Commitments Committee are noted at the E&S Committee and may include those subject to commitments in our E&S Framework.
