## **Supporting our large corporate and institutional customers to transition** 

We intend to support our large corporate and institutional banking customers in line with our credit policies, E&S Framework commitments, and sector-level financed emissions goals and targets. Our larger customers are generally more progressed on transitioning or adapting their businesses to make them more climate resilient. Our approach is focused on working with our customers to understand their long-term transition plans and funding needs. We provide financing solutions where it may assist their plans. 

## **Banking products, tools and services** 

We offer a range of financing solutions to support our customers in achieving their climate strategies and goals. We have a full range of financial markets capabilities, capital raising solutions, transaction banking services, and risk management products and services. Our sustainable finance team is dedicated to supporting customers in taking actions that drive better E&S outcomes, for example by linking their sustainability strategy to their financing arrangements. This includes structuring and facilitating use of proceeds instruments. Our carbon team also provides bespoke solutions to support project developers with managing price risk in the carbon market and supporting businesses to acquire or provide Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs).

## **Lending in line with our E&S Framework** 

The E&S Framework outlines our minimum requirements that seek to manage the climate impacts of our financing activities, including bond facilitation. This includes requirements for certain clients in the fossil fuel sectors, which can help us work towards our 2030 financed emissions targets for thermal coal mining, upstream oil and gas extraction and power generation. As set out in the E&S Framework, these requirements are subject to Australia having a secure energy platform. 

**See our refreshed 2025 E&S Framework at commbank.com.au/policies** 

## **Client Transition Plan assessments and criteria** 

In 2023 we clarified our expectations for certain clients to have a Transition Plan from 2025. 

This year this expectation applied to existing oil and/or gas producing or metallurgical coal mining clients who derive 15% or more of their revenue from the sale of oil, gas or metallurgical coal; or power generation clients who generate 25% or more of their electricity from coal, and to who we offer corporate or trade finance, or bond facilitation. We continued to engage a third party to independently assess applicable clients’ Transition Plans against the requirements set out in our E&S Framework, using the core criteria set out below. 

Unless we had already determined we would cease to offer new corporate or trade finance, or bond facilitation to a client from 1 January 2025, we continued to monitor the Transition Plans of applicable clients in FY25. In some instances, we determined the client would not have, or would be unlikely to have, a Transition Plan meeting our core criteria by 31 December 2024. After making this determination, we did not provide new corporate or trade finance, or bond facilitation with a maturity beyond 31 December 2024, except for uncommitted exposures that could be cancelled by 31 December 2024. After 1 January 2025, we did not provide new corporate or trade finance, or bond facilitation to clients whose Transition Plans did not meet the core criteria, based on the third-party assessment. We took the same approach for refinancing. Otherwise, existing exposures for these clients remain on our balance sheet beyond 31 December 2024 until their maturity. 

From the publication of our 2025 E&S Framework, our Transition Plan expectations will also apply to clients who derive 25% or more of their revenue from the sale of thermal coal, where we provide corporate or trade finance, or bond facilitation. 

**See our refreshed 2025 E&S Framework at commbank.com.au/policies**

Recognising that transition plan expectations are evolving, we also assess clients’ Transition Plans in relation to other matters. These matters can include, just transition, where the client’s Transition Plan recognises the social and economic impacts of its decarbonisation efforts, particularly the impact on communities with a heavy reliance on the fossil fuel industry; climate policy engagement; or TCFD disclosures, where the client explicitly signposts TCFD-aligned disclosures in its annual reporting or publishes them in a TCFD-aligned report.
