## **Lending to support the transition** continued 

This year we have taken the opportunity to clarify how we classify our aspirations as goals or targets. This resulted in some previously reported targets now being described as goals, supporting our continuous efforts to enhance the accuracy and transparency of our reporting. This change is intended to better align to where our pathways for achieving outcomes are subject to key external dependencies, for example the pace of Australia’s energy transition. We have set 10 goals and five targets covering 71% of CBA’s in-scope drawn lending. For several of these sectors, including Australian housing, Australian commercial property and aluminium, lower emissions are heavily reliant on grid decarbonisation. Should the electricity grid not decarbonise at the expected rate, or if State or Federal Government policy settings do not support decarbonisation, then achieving our goals for these sectors will be made more challenging. We continue to identify actions we can take to support our customers. 

## **Actions we are taking** 

Many factors can influence our financed emissions, including the composition of our lending portfolio, and our customers’ greenhouse gas emissions and activities. As we work towards our 2030 goals and targets, our reported figures may fluctuate up or down. Our approach to setting and achieving our goals and targets may also evolve as new data, methodologies and other factors emerge. Importantly, we do not expect our customers to follow the specific sector-level pathways we are working towards. 

## **How to read our sector-level charts** 

There are five key concepts to understand our sector-level financed emissions progress. 

- **1 Baseline:** reflects our portfolio’s starting point in terms of absolute emissions or emissions intensity. 

- **2 Actual:** our portfolio’s financed absolute emissions or emissions intensity for a given financial year. 

- **3 Reference scenario:** the science-based scenario we currently use to inform our goal or target and what we compare our portfolio’s financed absolute emissions or emissions intensity to. We expect to review these over time. 

## **There are three ways we are working towards our sector-level financed emissions goals and targets:** 

- **4 CBA portfolio:** an assessment of how the portfolio’s financed absolute emissions or emissions intensity estimate for the current reporting period compares to the reference scenario at the same point in time. 

- **5 Goal or target:** our financed absolute emissions or emissions intensity goal or target for the portfolio to be achieved by 2030. 

## **Taking steps that can help our customers reduce their emissions** 

For some sectors the charts do not include the baseline or actual to protect our customers’ confidentiality. This is to support cases where, for example, only one customer has drawn lending in the reporting period. 

Our approach varies by sector but can include engaging with customers, providing insights, data and dedicated products, services and tools, as well as advocating for government policies that can help them reduce their emissions. 

For some sectors, our baseline absolute emissions or emissions intensity is not the same as the reference scenario base year. For example, where our portfolio is not representative of the relevant Australian or global sector, to account for the impacts of COVID-19 on the sector, or due to data limitations.

## **Helping our retail customers** 

Australian consumers are increasingly investing in their own energy transitions with the installation of rooftop solar panels, battery storage and purchase of EVs. However, the costs to implement these technologies continues to be a barrier for many Australians who are seeking energy bill relief or fuel savings. Our approach is to help guide our customers by raising awareness of the technologies available, providing education, connecting customers with installers and EV manufacturers, and providing financing options and offers. 

With one in four Australian businesses naming CBA as their main financial institution, we aim to continue supporting small-to-medium businesses. We have a range of sustainable funding solutions to support our business customers to reduce their emissions, help our customers save on costs, build resilience and capture new growth opportunities. 

We offer tools which can help our customers identify opportunities to make sustainable business changes. Our Green Buildings Tool can help customers with commercial property identify actions which may improve energy efficiency and reduce emissions in their buildings. The tool estimates the upfront costs of implementing these actions, potential reduction in electricity costs upon implementation and the potential impact on the building’s National Australian Built Environment Rating System (NABERS) energy rating. Our Sustainability Action Tool offers insights, actions and resources to help small-tomedium businesses reduce environmental impacts and business costs, focusing on energy, transport and waste. 

Our Home Energy Upgrades service is designed to support customers through every step of their home energy upgrade journey, to help inform their decision making and understand potential energy bill savings. The service also connects customers with accredited solar and battery installers across Australia offering personalised quotes, along with discounted finance options and special offers. Our Home Energy Assessment Tool, developed in collaboration with Home Efficiency Australia, helps educate our customers by providing them an efficiency score and a cost-benefit breakdown for different home energy upgrades. 

To help simplify the car purchasing process for our customers we developed the Buy and Own a Car platform, enabling customers to purchase cars, including diesel, petrol and EVs. The platform encourages EV uptake by offering customers EV and charger discounts, an EV cost comparison tool, discounted finance, and educational resources to buy new and used cars. To support eligible essential workers and those earning under $100,000 per year, we launched the EV Access Program in partnership with the Clean Energy Finance Corporation (CEFC). This program offers lower-cost finance options for EVs under $55,000 enabling more customers to experience fuel savings through EVs. 

To better assist our customers with their sustainability journeys, we support our bankers with further learning, development and educational opportunities. This year Business Banking (BB) partnered with the Australian Graduate School of Management to deliver a ‘Future focused banking’ program. The program was completed by more than 150 of our approximately 800 frontline business bankers. It focused on improving banker knowledge and skills to discuss the opportunities and risks of the transition to a lower-carbon economy with our customers.
