## **Our approach to tax** 

**CBA is one of the largest taxpayers in Australia. We recognise the important contribution taxes make to support Government assets and services. Our approach to managing our tax affairs is in accordance with CBA’s values, purpose and strategy. Our commitment is to comply with prevailing tax laws in all jurisdictions that we operate in, and to maintain transparent and collaborative relationships with tax authorities.**

## **Position on political donations** 

**The Bank’s external communication and engagement policy prohibits political donations in the form of cash or money. We will pay to attend political events and forums. This year we spent $78,400 with the Australian Labor Party, $77,619 with the Liberal Party of Australia, and $18,500 with the National Party of Australia. These payments are disclosed to the Australian Electoral Commission in line with State and Federal regulation.** 

CBA has made a number of climate-related public commitments. These include supporting net zero by 2050 as part of collective efforts to limit global temperature rises. We have supported the development of a sustainable finance taxonomy and welcomed development of sectoral pathways, as well as supported the implementation of mandatory climate-related financial disclosures. These public commitments align with the Australian Banking Association and Business Council of Australia positions. 

CBA is a member of the Australian Sustainable Finance Institute, where we have contributed to its Taxonomy Technical Expert Group. We are also a member of the Green Building Council of Australia, which is focused on leading the sustainable transformation of the built environment, and the Electric Vehicle Council, which aims to increase certainty for investment through policy.

## **Working with our suppliers** 

Suppliers are important partners in delivering our strategy and managing our risks. We contract with over 3,100 Australian suppliers, including over 1,600 Australian small businesses. Indigenous-owned suppliers are providing products and services to the Bank and this year we spent over $62 million with Indigenous suppliers, up 177% compared to FY24. As part of our FY26-28 RAP, we are aiming to spend a cumulative $100 million with First Nations businesses over the next three years, with a focus on introducing new suppliers where practical.

## **Improving customer trust in banking** 

Banking is an essential service for customers, who depend on having secure and reliable access to cash and payment systems. CBA has a role to play in protecting our customers and contributing to financial system resilience. We understand the importance of continued investment and resources to keep our systems resilient, minimise disruption and protect customer data. However, as cyber threats grow more sophisticated and frequent, the risks to our customers, communities and operations continue to evolve. 

We are committed to helping protect our customers and the broader community from fraud, scams, cyber threats and financial crime. Doing so requires a whole-of-ecosystem approach. We are committed to working with governments, regulators, banks and other industries to support a national response.
