## **Sector overview and outlook** 

As at 30 June 2024, lending to the power generation sector represented only 0.5% of our in-scope drawn lending and 1.9% of our financed emissions. In 2023, Scope 1 emissions from the power generation sector accounted for approximately 34% of Australia’s total emissions.1 Retiring coal-fired power stations and shifting to renewable sources of energy is critical to reducing the emissions intensity of Australia’s electricity grid. Renewable electricity can provide a cost-effective way of reducing emissions for consumers and is not only essential for the nation to meet its decarbonisation ambitions, but also to support Australian households and other sectors in reducing their emissions. 

In the 2024 Integrated System Plan (ISP), AEMO’s step change scenario projects that up to 90% of the National Energy Market’s coal-fired power stations will retire by 2035 with the remaining 10% retiring by 2040. Renewable energy connected with transmission and distribution, firmed with storage and backed up by gas-powered generation, is the lowest-cost way to supply electricity to homes and businesses as Australia transitions to a net zero economy.2 

## **Our target, portfolio and progress** 

Our interim 2030 target is 105 kgCO2/MWh which is a 53% reduction compared to a 2020 baseline. As at 30 June 2024, the emissions intensity of our portfolio was 91 kgCO2/MWh, a reduction of 131 kgCO2/MWh compared to our 2020 baseline and 33 kgCO2/MWh compared to 30 June 2023. Our portfolio was also 73% below the reference scenario. The reduction in our portfolio emissions intensity was primarily driven by lower exposures to non-renewable power generation customers, new drawn lending to the renewables sector and changes in customer emissions. While the emissions intensity of our portfolio has continued to decrease since June 2020, drawdowns of unused lending limits could change our share of customers’ emissions and generation resulting in potential fluctuations in emissions intensity. 

Our power generation portfolio is diversified by technology and geography with over 50% of our lending exposures outside Australia. Our power generation portfolio is also 72% weighted to lending to solar and wind generation assets, 13% to other renewable generation assets, along with smaller drawn exposures to electricity generators and gentailers that are more emissions-intensive.

## **Assumptions and risks in achieving our target** 

The reference scenario that informs our target assumes a significant increase in renewable power generation from solar and wind, and a significant decrease in unabated fossil fuel generation by 2030. 

To maintain grid reliability and stability, and to reduce the sector’s emissions, investment in firming in the form of batteries, pumped hydro and some gas-fired electricity generation is crucial. We see a role for government policy and public funding to de-risk renewable energy and transmission projects. Should government policy settings change or not be implemented as currently stated, it may impact our ability to achieve our target. 

## **Actions we are taking** 

Our power generation portfolio includes customers ranging from lower emissions renewable energy generators to more emissions-intensive customers, such as electricity gentailers. In Australia and New Zealand, we are supporting our gentailer customers with financing as they transition to lower emissions technologies. We continue to focus on growing our lending to renewable energy and electricity storage customers to support the firming of renewables.
