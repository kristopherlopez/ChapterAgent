## **Engaging with our regulators** 

We maintain transparent and constructive relationships with both financial and non-financial regulators to support strong and consistent outcomes for our customers and the broader community. CBA engages proactively with regulators to listen to feedback and respond in a timely manner to meet our regulatory engagement principles and obligations. 

Domestic financial regulators play an important role in maintaining the stability, fairness and efficiency of Australia’s financial system. A strong financial system protects consumers and contributes to the country’s prosperity. Trust and confidence in our financial system attracts investment in Australian banks, enabling us to lend to Australian households and businesses. 

Globally, bank deregulation has emerged as a significant topic. Domestically, there is increasing investor interest in the impact of regulation on housing affordability, access to credit, regional banking, and fraud and scams. Australia’s regulation has served our economy well through uncertain times. Financial regulators have a key role in considering regulatory reforms that could address domestic issues such as housing affordability and access to banking services. CBA aims to engage with regulators on key issues, and share our customer insights and other relevant perspectives.

## **Improving payments regulation** 

**Delivering a safe, reliable and efficient payments system is a regulatory priority. We welcomed the RBA review of merchant card payment costs and surcharging as an opportunity to deliver better outcomes for consumers and small businesses. Customer preferences towards contactless payments and ecommerce is growing with electronic payments being the lowest cost form of payment to accept for merchants.** 

**We recognise that it is in Australia’s national interest to have an Australian bank providing banking services in the Pacific, helping to promote social and economic integration. This year CBA committed to opening a banking agency in Nauru to provide limited transaction, savings and merchant services. CBA is working closely with the Australian Government and the Government of Nauru to deliver a smooth transition of services later this calendar year.** 

**It is important that payment regulation applies to popular payment solutions such as digital wallets and buy-now-pay-later services. To simplify the system, we support removing surcharging for both credit and debit cards with safeguards so that small businesses are supported and benefit from lower costs. With interchange fees supporting a range of benefits valued by customers and merchants, appropriate pricing and economics across the payments ecosystem is essential for a safe, reliable, sustainable and innovative payments system.** 

**The interaction between these policy changes and proposed legislative changes to the** _**Payment Systems (Regulation) Act 1998**_ **are important and complex. The RBA is consulting to ensure there are no unintended consequences and any changes are in the best interest of Australia’s payments ecosystem and end users.**

## **Fostering a safe workplace**

Our focus on the health, safety and wellbeing of our people helps them reach their full potential. It also reflects the right of all people to work in an environment that is free from unlawful workplace conduct, such as discrimination, harassment, sexual or sex-based harassment, bullying and victimisation. We have always taken our obligations to keep our people safe seriously and the positive duty for organisations is aligned to our approach. 

A range of key programs and processes help us maintain a safe workplace. This includes our ‘Respect Lives Here’ initiative and our health and safety management system. ‘Respect Lives Here’ is our prevention initiative focused on building a shared understanding of respectful and disrespectful behaviours at work. This year we again partnered with the University of New South Wales to survey all of our people, listen to their experiences, and provide practical guidance on becoming active bystanders when something does not feel right. As part of our prevention strategy, our education and training programs help people understand our expectations of appropriate workplace behaviour. This includes how to identify, report and address conduct-related issues. 

The Bank expects appropriate standards of conduct in accordance with our Code of Conduct at all times. We take appropriate disciplinary action in relation to any breaches of our Conduct Policy, the Code of Conduct or our values, up to and including dismissal from employment.

## **Speaking up** 

**We strive to create a work environment that promotes the right behaviours. Providing our SpeakUP services supports our people and external partners to raise concerns safely, including anonymously if desired. Support and protection are provided to whistleblowers under the Group Whistleblower Policy and applicable laws.** 

**The Audit Committee and an executive committee receive reporting on the operation of SpeakUP three times a year and quarterly, respectively. This year 408 reports were made to the SpeakUP Program, up from 331 in 2024. Of these reports, 68 were whistleblower disclosures.**
