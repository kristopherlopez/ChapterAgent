## **Our emerging risks** 

While remaining vigilant to the risks we need to manage today, we also look ahead to identify potential future challenges. We aim to uphold the risk management standards expected by our customers, communities, regulators and shareholders, both now and in the future. 

Emerging risks are risks that are either newly developing or already exist, but their effects have not yet been substantially realised. These risks are often constantly evolving and have the potential to impact the Bank and our customers in the medium to long term (over 12 months). It is essential to respond to these risks now to minimise their potential future impact. Emerging risks are often driven by new trends in our operating environment, such as competition, new technologies, regulation or evolving customer expectations. 

Our emerging risk profile is updated annually through a review process with the ELT and the Board. This process aligns with the annual review of the Bank’s strategy. The emerging risk process evaluates the adequacy of the Bank’s mitigation strategies to prevent emerging risks from materially impacting the Bank. In cases where we may have limited ability to influence emerging risks, it may be more challenging to implement effective mitigative actions. This may require us to take additional steps to address these risks. 

The emerging risk themes the Board and ELT focused on this year are outlined below.

## **Regulatory reforms** 

## **macroeconomic uncertainty** 

Upcoming regulatory reforms could significantly impact us and our customers, spanning areas like Anti-Money Laundering/Counter-Terrorism Financing (AML/CTF), cyber security, scams, privacy, payments licensing, AI and climate disclosures. To prepare for new regulation, we frequently evaluate our obligations including those under the new _Scam Prevention Framework Act 2025 (Cth)_ , which will be further supported by industry specific codes, not yet released. The Bank is also improving its security infrastructure for fraud and scams, and forming partnerships to better handle scam phishing and reporting within the industry ecosystem. 

Our operations face risks from global political events, such as elections and policy changes, civil unrest, international competition and conflicts. These disruptions can increase social pressures domestically; affect unemployment, housing supply, and market volatility; and escalate credit and operational risks for financial institutions. Failure to prepare for these geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainties could heighten our exposure to various financial and non-financial risks, which we monitor and manage through our RMF. 

## **Energy transition in Australia** 

## **Emerging technology** 

Australia’s economy needs affordable and reliable energy. Replacing coal-fired power with firmed renewable energy is crucial to meet Australia’s renewable energy targets. With 90% of coal-fired power stations expected to retire by 2035, delays in replacement generation could increase electricity price volatility, affect reliability and have broader economic impacts. Credit risks could also be heightened for the Bank, as well as disruptions to our operations. We continue to engage with Government on the energy transition and support retail and business customers finance existing technologies, such as rooftop solar, batteries and electric vehicles to help reduce their emissions. This also helps reduce reliance on large-scale utility investments. 

Innovation driven by emerging and disruptive technology, including AI, continues to rapidly evolve. As we pursue our strategy, which includes a focus on technology and AI, we continue working hard to effectively manage risks from change implementation that could lead to service disruptions, poor customer experiences, fraud and scams, conduct issues, cyber security threats, and data and privacy issues. As a Bank, we invest in governance and risk initiatives to support our AI goals, including for responsible AI. 

## **Cyber security threats** 

The Bank anticipates cyber attacks to persist and grow as adversaries use new technologies such as GenAI to exploit vulnerabilities, including third parties and cloud environments. These could lead to compromised customer data, critical systems disruptions and potential breaches of our obligations. We continue to strengthen our cyber security risk management through dedicated teams that monitor and respond to potential cyber security threats, applying learnings from the Board and senior management crisis management exercises, and a focus on enhancing our cyber security control environment to address emerging threats. 

## **Growing societal pressures impacting our customers and communities** 

The Bank continues to face heightened expectations from governments, regulators and the community to respond to key challenges including cost-of-living pressures, housing affordability and crisis support. Many Australians are feeling the impact of rising fraud and scams activity, as well as ongoing affordability concerns. We remain focused on supporting our customers through these challenges and continue to invest in strengthening our fraud and scams infrastructure to help keep them safe. 

## **Our material risks** 

Our material risks are those the Bank places extra focus on to mitigate, as they could significantly impact the Bank, our customers, communities and shareholders in the near term. 

The Bank faces various risks through the products and services we offer. We categorise these risks as strategic, financial and non-financial based on the nature of their impacts. Each year the Board approves the material ‘Level 1’ risk types in the RMF, which establishes the risks needing immediate management and control. The material risks outlined below have been our primary focus this year.
