## 1. Purpose

This standard establishes the data governance requirements for all data used in, generated by, or associated with artificial intelligence and machine learning systems across the PetSure Australia Group. It ensures that data used in AI systems meets the Group's requirements for quality, privacy, security, lineage, and regulatory compliance.

AI systems are fundamentally dependent on data. The quality, representativeness, and governance of that data directly determines the quality, fairness, and safety of AI outputs. Poor data governance in AI systems creates risks that are amplified by the scale and speed at which AI operates: a bias in training data becomes a bias in millions of automated decisions; a PII leak in a prompt response is replicated across every similar interaction.

This standard is subordinate to the PetSure Australia Group AI Policy (GOV-AI-001) and implements the data-related governance requirements referenced in that policy. It should be read in conjunction with the PetSure Australia Model Risk Management Framework (GOV-AI-003) for model-specific data requirements and the PetSure Australia AI Testing & Evaluation Framework (GOV-AI-006) for evaluation data requirements.

## 2. Scope

This standard applies to:

- All data used to train, fine-tune, or calibrate AI and machine learning models.
- All data used in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) knowledge bases and vector stores.
- All data used in golden datasets for evaluation and compliance testing.
- All data generated by AI systems, including model outputs, predictions, classifications, and generated text.
- All interaction data, including user queries, AI responses, trace data, and audit logs.
- All metadata associated with AI systems, including solution manifests, evaluation results, and compliance evidence.

This standard does **not** replace the Group's existing data governance policies. It provides AI-specific requirements that supplement and extend those policies. Where this standard imposes requirements beyond general data governance, the stricter requirement applies.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Personal Information (PI)** | Information or an opinion about an identified individual, or an individual who is reasonably identifiable, whether the information is true or not and whether recorded in a material form or not. Defined under the Australian Privacy Act 1988. |
| **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)** | A subset of personal information that can be used to uniquely identify an individual. Includes direct identifiers (name, address, email) and indirect identifiers that in combination could identify an individual. |
| **Sensitive Information** | A subset of personal information defined in the Privacy Act 1988 that attracts additional protections: racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation, criminal record, health information, genetic information, biometric data, and trade union membership. |
| **Data Lineage** | A documented record of data's origins, transformations, and movements from source systems through to its use in an AI system. Lineage enables traceability and impact analysis. |
| **Data Classification** | The assignment of a sensitivity level to data that determines the security controls and handling requirements applied to it. |
| **Synthetic Data** | Data that is artificially generated to resemble real data in structure and statistical properties but does not correspond to any real individual or transaction. |
| **Golden Dataset** | A curated, human-reviewed set of test cases used to evaluate an AI solution's quality, safety, and compliance, as defined in GOV-AI-001 Section 8.1. |

## 4. Data Classification Scheme

### 4.1 Classification Levels

All data associated with AI systems must be classified according to the Group's four-tier data classification scheme:

| Classification | Definition | Examples in AI Context |
|---------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Public** | Information that is intended for or available to the public. Disclosure causes no harm. | Published AI principles, public-facing documentation, open-source model architectures |
| **Internal** | Information intended for use within PetSure Australia. Disclosure could cause minor reputational impact. | Internal policy documents, non-sensitive training data, aggregated performance metrics, solution manifests |
| **Confidential** | Information that could cause material harm to PetSure Australia or its customers if disclosed. | Customer interaction logs, model outputs containing business logic, evaluation results, detailed model architectures, competitive information |
| **Restricted** | Information that could cause severe harm if disclosed. Subject to the strictest access controls. | Raw customer PII, credit scoring model weights, fraud detection model parameters, encryption keys, regulatory submissions |

### 4.2 AI Data Type Classification

The following table provides default classifications for common AI data types. Solution owners may elevate (but not lower) these classifications based on their specific context.

| Data Type | Default Classification | Notes |
|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| Solution manifests (`solution.yaml`) | Internal | May be elevated if manifest contains sensitive configuration |
| Knowledge base documents (RAG corpus) | Depends on source | Inherits classification of source documents |
| Training data (anonymised) | Confidential | Even anonymised training data reveals modelling approach |
| Training data (containing PII) | Restricted | Must be processed per Section 6 before use |
| Golden datasets | Internal | Must not contain real PII (see Section 5.2) |
| Evaluation results | Confidential | Reveals model capabilities and limitations |
| User interaction logs | Confidential | May contain customer queries with sensitive information |
| AI-generated responses | Confidential | May contain synthesised information from confidential sources |
| Trace data (audit trail) | Confidential | Contains full interaction chain including intermediate reasoning |
| Model weights and parameters | Restricted | Core intellectual property; competitive risk if disclosed |
| Compliance evidence packages | Confidential | Contains governance assessment results |

### 4.3 Handling Requirements by Classification

| Requirement | Internal | Confidential | Restricted |
|-------------|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Access control | Role-based | Role-based + need-to-know | Named individuals + approval |
| Encryption at rest | Required | Required (AES-256) | Required (AES-256 + key management) |
| Encryption in transit | Required (TLS 1.2+) | Required (TLS 1.3) | Required (TLS 1.3 + certificate pinning) |
| Logging of access | Recommended | Required | Required (with alerting) |
| Data loss prevention (DLP) | Standard controls | Enhanced DLP rules | Strictest DLP + monitoring |
| Sharing outside PetSure Australia | Permitted with approval | Permitted with CDO approval | Not permitted without GCRO approval |

## 5. Training Data Requirements

### 5.1 Data Quality Standards

All training data must meet the following quality requirements before being used to train or fine-tune a model:

- **Completeness** — missing values must be documented and handled appropriately. If more than 10% of a critical feature is missing, the data source must be reviewed before use.
- **Accuracy** — training data must be validated against source systems. Stale data (more than 12 months old for customer behavioural data) must be flagged and assessed for continued relevance.
- **Consistency** — data from multiple sources must be reconciled. Conflicts between sources must be resolved and documented.
- **Representativeness** — training data must be assessed for demographic representativeness. If the training population differs materially from the intended deployment population, this must be documented in the model card (GOV-AI-003) and addressed through sampling, reweighting, or other techniques.
- **Bias assessment** — all training data used for Tier 2 and Tier 3 solutions must undergo a documented bias assessment before model training. The assessment must evaluate the distribution of outcomes across protected attributes (age, gender, postcode as a proxy for socioeconomic status, indigenous status) and document any identified disparities.

### 5.2 Evaluation Data Requirements

Golden datasets used for evaluation and compliance testing have specific requirements that differ from training data:

- **Synthetic data mandate** — golden datasets must use synthetic data. Real customer data, including anonymised customer data, must not be used in golden datasets. This requirement is absolute and applies to all risk tiers.
- **Realistic representation** — while synthetic, golden dataset test cases must be realistic and representative of actual production scenarios, including edge cases and adversarial inputs.
- **No data leakage** — golden dataset test cases must not overlap with training data. If a model has been trained on a knowledge base, golden dataset questions must test the model's ability to use that knowledge rather than simply regurgitating memorised responses.
- **Minimum case counts** — as defined in GOV-AI-001 Section 5.2: 30+ cases for `production_internal`, 50+ cases for `production_customer_facing`.
- **Case type coverage** — golden datasets must include cases covering: expected queries, edge cases, out-of-scope queries (to test boundary adherence), adversarial inputs (prompt injection, jailbreak attempts), and PII-probing queries.

### 5.3 RAG Knowledge Base Data

Data ingested into retrieval-augmented generation knowledge bases must:

- Be classified and handled according to its original classification level.
- Have documented lineage from source to vector store, including any transformations (chunking, embedding, summarisation).
- Be reviewed for accuracy and currency before ingestion. Knowledge bases must have a defined refresh cadence.
- Not contain PII unless the solution is specifically authorised to process PII and appropriate guardrails (AI-GOV-005) are configured.
- Be scoped to the solution's authorised domain. Knowledge base content that is outside the solution's declared scope increases the risk of off-topic or inappropriate responses.

## 6. PII Handling

### 6.1 PII Definition for AI Systems

For the purposes of this standard, PII includes any information that could identify an individual, either directly or in combination with other information available to the AI system. The following categories are explicitly in scope:

**Direct Identifiers**

| PII Type | Description | Australian-Specific Notes |
|----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Full name | Given name + surname, or either in context | Common names may require combination with other identifiers |
| Email address | Personal or professional email | |
| Phone number | Mobile, landline, or fax | Australian format: 04xx xxx xxx, (0x) xxxx xxxx |
| Physical address | Street address, including partial addresses | |
| Date of birth | Full or partial (day + month, or year) | |
| Tax File Number (TFN) | 9-digit Australian tax identifier | Regulated under Tax File Number Guidelines 2015; must never appear in AI outputs |
| Medicare number | 10-11 digit health identifier | Regulated under Healthcare Identifiers Act 2010 |
| Australian Business Number (ABN) | 11-digit business identifier | May identify sole traders as individuals |
| Driver's licence number | State-issued licence number | Format varies by state/territory |
| Passport number | Australian or foreign passport | |
| Bank account details | BSB + account number | PetSure Australia-specific: 06xxxx BSB range |
| Credit card number | Full or partial card number | PCI DSS requirements apply in addition to this standard |
| Customer Reference Number (CRN) | PetSure Australia internal customer identifier | Restricted — must not appear in AI outputs or logs |

**Indirect Identifiers (in combination)**

Combinations of the following may constitute PII when they could identify an individual in context: postcode + age + gender, occupation + employer + suburb, claim details + date + location.

### 6.2 PII Detection Requirements

All AI solutions that process text or unstructured data must implement PII detection guardrails as part of the platform's guardrail validation gate (AI-GOV-006). Requirements by tier:

| Requirement | Experimental | Production Internal | Production Customer-Facing |
|-------------|-------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| PII detection in outputs | Recommended | Required | Required (zero tolerance) |
| PII detection in inputs | Not required | Recommended | Required |
| PII detection method | Any | Regex + NER model | Regex + NER model + LLM-based review |
| TFN-specific detection | Required if processing financial data | Required | Required |
| Medicare-specific detection | Not required | Required if processing health data | Required |
| PII detection logging | Recommended | Required | Required |
| False positive review | Not required | Quarterly | Monthly |

### 6.3 PII in AI Interactions

When PII is detected in an AI interaction:

- **In AI outputs** — the response must be blocked and the interaction logged as a PII incident. The solution owner must be notified. For customer-facing solutions, this triggers an immediate investigation per GOV-AI-001 Section 9.
- **In user inputs** — the PII should be redacted from logs and trace data where possible. If the PII is necessary for the AI to complete its function (e.g., a customer providing their name to verify identity), the solution must have explicit authorisation to process that PII type, and the interaction must be handled at the Confidential classification level or above.

## 7. Data Retention

### 7.1 Retention Periods

| Data Type | Retention Period | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Training data (raw) | Lifetime of model + 5 years | Required for model re-validation and regulatory enquiry |
| Training data (processed/feature-engineered) | Lifetime of model + 3 years | Required for model reproducibility |
| RAG knowledge base content | Current version + 2 prior versions | Required for audit trail of information available to the AI |
| Golden datasets | Lifetime of solution + 5 years | Required for compliance evidence |
| Evaluation results | Lifetime of solution + 5 years | Required for compliance evidence |
| User interaction logs | 7 years | Aligned to APRA record-keeping requirements and Australian financial services regulations |
| Trace data (full audit trail) | 7 years | Required for reconstructing AI-assisted decisions per GOV-AI-001 Section 4.5 |
| AI-generated responses | 7 years (customer-facing), 3 years (internal) | Customer-facing: regulatory; internal: operational |
| Compliance evidence packages | 7 years from date of evidence | APRA audit requirements |
| Model weights and checkpoints | Lifetime of model + 2 years | Required for incident investigation and reproducibility |
| Solution manifests (historical versions) | Lifetime of solution + 5 years | Required for governance audit trail |

### 7.2 Retention Enforcement

Retention periods are minimums. Data must not be retained beyond the stated period plus a 90-day grace period unless a legal hold or regulatory investigation requires extended retention.

The Chief Data Office audits retention compliance annually. Non-compliant retention (both premature deletion and excessive retention) is reported as a governance finding.

## 8. Data Lineage

### 8.1 Lineage Requirements

Data lineage must be documented for all data used in Tier 2 and Tier 3 AI solutions. Lineage documentation must trace data from its source system through all transformations to its point of use in the AI system.

Required lineage elements:

| Element | Description | Required From |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Source system | The originating system or data store | Tier 2+ |
| Source owner | The team or individual responsible for the source data | Tier 2+ |
| Extraction method | How data was extracted (API, database query, file transfer, manual) | Tier 2+ |
| Transformation steps | Each transformation applied to the data (cleaning, normalisation, encoding, embedding) | Tier 2+ |
| Quality checks applied | Data quality validations performed at each stage | Tier 2+ |
| Destination | Where the data is stored or used (training pipeline, vector store, evaluation harness) | Tier 2+ |
| Refresh frequency | How often the data is updated from source | Tier 2+ |
| Last refresh date | When the data was last updated | Tier 2+ |

### 8.2 Lineage for RAG Systems

Retrieval-augmented generation systems require additional lineage documentation:

- **Document-level lineage** — each document in the knowledge base must be traceable to its source, including the version of the source document, the date it was ingested, and any transformations applied (chunking strategy, metadata extraction).
- **Retrieval lineage** — the platform's trace data must capture which documents were retrieved for each query, enabling audit of the information basis for any AI response.
- **Refresh lineage** — when knowledge base content is updated, the lineage must capture what changed, when, and why.

## 9. Cross-Border Data Restrictions

### 9.1 Data Sovereignty

Australian customer data processed by AI systems must remain within Australian borders unless an explicit exception is approved. This applies to:

- Training data containing or derived from Australian customer information.
- User interaction logs from Australian customers.
- AI-generated outputs that contain or are derived from Australian customer data.
- RAG knowledge bases that contain Australian customer information.

### 9.2 Cloud and API Considerations

Many AI systems rely on cloud-hosted models and APIs. The following requirements apply:

| Scenario | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| PetSure Australia-hosted model in Australian data centre | Permitted — preferred approach |
| Cloud-hosted model in Australian region (e.g., AWS Sydney, Azure Australia East) | Permitted — data residency must be contractually guaranteed |
| Cloud-hosted model outside Australia (e.g., OpenAI API, Anthropic API) | Restricted — permitted only if: (a) no Australian customer PII is transmitted, (b) data is anonymised or synthetic, (c) approved by the Chief Data Officer, and (d) a Data Processing Agreement is in place |
| Third-party model accessed via API with no data residency guarantee | Not permitted for Confidential or Restricted data |

### 9.3 Embedding and Vector Storage

When customer data is transformed into embeddings (vector representations), it retains its original classification and data sovereignty requirements. Embeddings are not considered anonymised because:

- Embedding inversion attacks may partially reconstruct original content.
- Nearest-neighbour searches in embedding space may reveal information about the original data.
- The combination of embeddings with metadata may enable re-identification.

Vector stores containing embeddings derived from Australian customer data must be hosted in Australia.

## 10. Right to Explanation

### 10.1 Data Subject Rights

When an AI system makes or materially contributes to a decision about an individual, that individual has the right to understand the basis of the decision. This is derived from:

- Australian Privacy Principle 6 (use and disclosure of personal information).
- ASIC Regulatory Guide 209 (credit licensing obligations, including explainability of credit decisions).
- PetSure Australia's Responsible AI Principles (GOV-AI-002).

### 10.2 Explanation Requirements by Decision Type

| Decision Type | Explanation Requirement | Explanation Method |
|--------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Credit decisioning (approve/decline) | Mandatory — must be provided to applicant on request | Key factors driving the decision, presented in plain language |
| Credit limit or pricing determination | Mandatory — must be available for customer enquiry | Primary variables and their directional influence |
| Insurance claim assessment | Mandatory — must be provided with claim outcome | Factors considered and their contribution to the assessment |
| Customer service triage or routing | Low — informational only | General description of routing logic |
| Internal risk assessment | Medium — available to affected employee on request | Key factors and data sources used |
| Fraud detection (alert generation) | Not provided to subject (operational security) | Available to internal investigators on request |

### 10.3 Explanation Data Requirements

To support the right to explanation, solutions must retain:

- The input data used for the specific decision (not just aggregated data).
- The model version and configuration active at the time of the decision.
- The output and any intermediate reasoning steps (trace data).
- The guardrail results for the interaction.

This data must be retained for 7 years in accordance with Section 7.1.
