# Research — PetSure Australia Head of

## PetSure Australia Agile Operating Model

PetSure Australia uses a Spotify-inspired agile model. Key distinction:

### Head of (this role)
- **People leader** — owns a discipline (e.g., data science) across multiple teams
- Responsible for hiring, coaching, career development, capability building
- Sets technical standards and best practices for the team
- Team members are "on loan" to teams but report to the Head of
- Think: **functional manager** — you own the people and the craft

### Team Lead
- **Delivery leader** — owns a product or mission outcome
- Leads a cross-functional team (engineers, designers, data scientists, etc.)
- Responsible for backlog, priorities, shipping outcomes
- Borrows people from teams to staff the team
- Think: **product/project owner** — you own the what and the when

### Day-to-day differences

| | Head of | Team Lead |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning focus** | Checking in on people across multiple teams — coaching conversations, unblocking capability gaps, reviewing technical quality | Standup with your team — what shipped, what's blocked, what's next |
| **Meetings** | 1:1s with team members, hiring panels, capability reviews, community of practice sessions | Sprint ceremonies, stakeholder demos, backlog refinement, cross-team dependencies |
| **Decisions you make** | Who to hire, who to promote, what training to invest in, technical standards to enforce | What to build next, how to sequence work, when to cut scope |
| **What keeps you up** | "Are my people growing? Is the craft improving? Do teams have what they need?" | "Are we shipping? Are stakeholders happy? Is the backlog healthy?" |
| **Stakeholders** | Your team members, team leads (as peers), GM, HR/talent | Product owners, business stakeholders, team leads (for resourcing) |
| **Rhythm** | Longer cycles — quarterly capability plans, annual talent reviews, ongoing coaching | Sprint cadence — fortnightly delivery, regular retros, continuous reprioritisation |
| **Conflict type** | Team lead wants your best person full-time; you need to rotate for growth. Balancing individual development against delivery pressure | Scope creep from stakeholders; trade-offs between speed and quality; dependency on other teams |
| **Visible output** | Healthier team, better hiring pipeline, rising technical bar, people getting promoted | Features shipped, metrics moved, stakeholder problems solved |
| **Power source** | Expertise and trust — people follow you because you make them better | Delivery authority — you control priorities and the path to production |

### A typical month as Head of

**Week 1 — People rhythm**
- 1:1s with each team member (10-20 people across multiple teams). How's the work? Where are you stuck? What do you want to learn?
- Catch up with team leads — are my people performing? Any capability gaps showing up?
- Review open hiring reqs — screen CVs, run technical panels

**Week 2 — Craft & standards**
- Community of practice session — someone presents their model, peer review, knowledge sharing
- Review technical work in progress — model validation approaches, code quality, documentation
- Update or refine team standards — versioning, testing frameworks, documentation expectations
- Meet with platform/MLOps teams on tooling decisions

**Week 3 — Strategy & stakeholders**
- Capability planning — do we have the right skills for what's coming next quarter? Where are the gaps?
- GM catch-up — aligning on headcount, budget, strategic priorities
- Cross-team coordination — connecting with other team leads (engineering, design) on shared problems
- APRA/model risk governance — making sure the team's output meets regulatory expectations

**Week 4 — Talent & growth**
- Career development conversations — promotions, moves, stretch assignments
- Workforce planning — which teams need more capacity? Who's ready to rotate?
- Training investment decisions — conferences, courses, certifications
- Retrospective on team health — attrition risk, engagement, delivery quality

**Running through every week:**
- Unblocking people — someone's stuck on a team that isn't working, a team lead is frustrated with output, someone wants to move teams
- Hiring — always in some stage if you're growing
- Fielding requests from team leads who want more or different people

The rhythm is longer than a team lead's. They live in fortnightly sprints. You live in quarterly capability cycles with weekly people touchpoints.

### Technical authority (beyond coaching)
- **Setting standards** — what "good" looks like for data science across all teams
- **Technical governance** — reviewing and approving approaches before production
- **Tooling & platform decisions** — owning the data science tech stack choices
- **Community of practice** — scaling knowledge across the team
- **Hiring bar** — defining technical assessment criteria
- **Architecture input** — team leads decide *what* to build, you have a strong voice in *how*

### Team lead profile
- In Risk Management, team leads are more likely **domain people** — risk analysts, credit risk specialists, actuaries — not data scientists
- The team exists to solve a business problem; the team lead understands the problem and manages stakeholders
- Exception: platform-oriented teams (e.g. MLOps) may have engineering-background team leads
- Team leads have **no direct reports** — team members report to you, not to them
- Key implication: your relationship with team leads is a negotiation, not a hierarchy

### Why it matters for this role
- You won't own a product backlog — you own the **data science capability** across Risk Management
- Your people will be embedded in teams but you're accountable for their growth, quality, and technical direction
- Influence is indirect: you shape how AI gets done, team leads shape what gets built
- This is a strength play for Kris — building teams and lifting capability is the PetSure story
